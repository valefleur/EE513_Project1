Compute your pixel pitch in micrometers as 1000 fmm / fpx, where fmm is the focal length recorded in the EXIF metadata and fpx is the mean of the two focal lengths in pixels from item 1. 

EXIF data says focal length is fmm = 6.8 mm
In the intrinsic characteristics K matrix determined by cv.calibrateCameraExtended() on Oct. 12th,
f_x = 2884 px
f_y = 2871 px
fpx = (2884 + 2871) / 2 = 2878 px

pixel pitch = 6.8 mm / 2878 px * 1000 = 2.363 um/px


Then obtain it a second way, by a route that does not use your calibration at all: 
1. divide the 35 mm equivalent focal length in the EXIF by the actual focal length to get the crop factor, 
2. divide the 43.3 mm diagonal of 35 mm film by the crop factor to get your sensor diagonal, 
3. and divide that by the image diagonal in pixels. 

1. EXIF data says the 35 mm equivalent focal length is 24.0 mm and the actual is 6.8 mm.
	24.0 mm / 6.8 mm = 3.529 crop factor
2. 43.3 mm diag / 3.529 crop factor = 12.270 sensor diagonal
3. image diagonal in pixels = hypotenuse = np.sqrt(width**2 + height**2) = np.sqrt(3024**2 + 4032**2) = 5040 px

	pixel pitch = 12.270 mm / 5040 px =  2.434 um/px

RMS = np.sqrt((2.363**2 + 2.434**2)/ 2) = np.sqrt(11.508 / 2) = np.sqrt(5.754) = 2.40
	(2.434 − 2.363) / 2.40 * 100 = 2.96%

Everything in Part 2 rests on this number, and the second route is the only warning you will get that the first one is wrong.
